How practice works

One passage at a time.

  1. 01

    Slow it down

    Play anywhere from 30 to 130% speed. The pitch stays the same, so your ear keeps up.

  2. 02

    Repeat until it’s clean

    Pick a range of bars and repeat it. Turn on gradual speed to go faster after each clean pass.

  3. 03

    Follow every note

    The note you should play turns red, and the page scrolls with you. The rhythm sits right under the tab.

                    Reading your first tab

                    Each horizontal line is a string. The top line is the highest-sounding string. A number tells you which fret to press. A zero means an open string.

                    G |-------------|
D |-------------|
A |-----3-------|
E |-0-------3---|

                    Read from left to right: play the open E string, fret 3 on the A string, then fret 3 on the E string.

                    Press Play to hear the rhythm. Start at 50% speed, follow the highlighted notes, and repeat a few bars until they feel comfortable.

                    Writing a song

                    A song is plain text: a few header lines, then bars of notes separated by |.

                    title: My groove
artist: Jane Doe, John Roe  # optional: who wrote it
about: Eighth notes on the root  # optional: what the song trains
listen: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=...  # optional: the original
instrument: bass4      # bass4, bass5, bass6, guitar6
tuning: D2 A2 D3 G3    # optional: any tuning, low to high
tempo: 90
key: C                 # C, Ab, F#m, ...
time: 4/4

@Verse                 # section name
"C" :q 3.3 3.3 :8 5.2 5.2 :q 3.3 |
(3.3 5.2):h r:h |      # two notes together, then a rest
[3/4] :8t 3.3 5.3 7.3 :q ~7.3 r |
                    artist:who wrote the song, shown as "by ...". Separate several names with commas
                    about:one line on what the song trains, shown under the title
                    level:easy, medium or hard. Put it under a == Name line to rate one version
                    listen:web address of a recording of the original, shown as a "Listen to the original" link
                    3.4fret 3 on string 4 (string 1 is the highest)
                    :qduration for the next notes: w h q 8 16 32. Add . for dotted and t for triplet
                    3.4:8duration for one note
                    (3.4 5.3)notes played together
                    rrest
                    ~5.3tie: keep the note ringing without picking it again
                    "Am7"chord name above the next note
                    {let ring}text above the next note (dynamics, tips)
                    <la la la>words to sing, below the tab from the next note
                    [3/4]time signature from this bar on
                    [tempo=130]quarter-note tempo from this bar on, before its first note
                    @Chorussection name, on its own line
                    == Bass linestarts another version of the song, on its own line. Versions share the header. Switch between them with the buttons under the title
                    # ...comment

                    Jam

                    Open Jam from the top bar to improvise over a looping groove of drums, bass and keys. Pick a groove from the list, press Play or Space, then choose a tempo and a key. The chord names follow the key, and the outlined bar is the one you hear. Changes are heard from the next bar.

                    Progression swaps the groove's chords for another set, such as a 12 bar blues or ii V I. Swing moves the off beat notes from straight to a full triplet. The app remembers tempo, key, progression and swing for each groove.

                    In the mixer, choose what you play. Bass mutes the backing bass so you take its place. Each part has its own mute button and volume. Drum room adds a short room echo to the drums. The app remembers the mixer for all grooves.

                    Count-in plays one bar of clicks before the loop starts, at the click volume from the practice Timing menu. Copy link copies an address that opens the groove with your key, tempo, progression and swing.

                    Writing a groove

                    A groove is plain text: the same header lines as a song, then one line of steps per part.

                    In Jam, My grooves has Edit text, New and Open file. The groove text opens above the chords. While every line reads cleanly, the loop plays what you type from the next bar; while a line has a problem, the list names its line and the last working version keeps playing. Save keeps the groove in this browser under Mine in the groove list. Download saves it as a text file, and Delete removes a saved groove after asking.

                    title: My shuffle
genre: blues
about: A twelve bar shuffle to practise blues lines.
tempo: 80
key: A                 # the chords follow any key
time: 4/4
swing: 66              # 50 is straight, 66 is a triplet shuffle, up to 75
progression: I7 IV7 I7 I7 | IV7 IV7 I7 I7 | V7 IV7 I7 V7
voice: keys            # keys, organ or drive
kit: blues             # rock, blues, funk, reggae, jazz, metal or electronic
fill: on               # a tom fill at the end of the progression, off by default

hat:   xoXoxoXo        # one bar of 8 steps, repeated every bar
snare: ..X...X. | ..X..oX.
kick:  X...x... | X...x..o
keys:  ..X-..X-
bass:  1 - 3 - 5 - 6 - | b7 - 6 - 5 - >b1 -
                    genre:the style shown in the groove list, such as blues, rock, reggae or funk
                    about:one line on what the groove practises, shown under the title
                    swing:how late the off beat notes land: 50 is straight, 66 is a triplet, up to 75. The Jam screen starts from this value, and its swing slider stops at the triplet
                    progression:the chords the groove plays by default. The Jam screen can swap them for another progression
                    voice:the sound of the keys part: keys (electric piano, the default), organ, or drive, a distorted voice for power chords
                    kit:the drum sound and feel: rock (the default), blues, funk, reggae, jazz, metal or electronic. Each kit tunes the drums and how loosely they sit on the beat
                    fill:on plays a tom fill over the last two beats of the progression, in place of the drum lines there. off is the default
                    I7 iv bVIIone chord per bar as a roman numeral: upper case is major, lower case is minor, b or # shifts it. Numerals count from the major scale of the key's root, so a minor key uses i iv v bIII bVI bVII
                    7 maj7 m7 m7b5 dim sus4 5chord qualities after the numeral
                    kick snare rim hat halfhat openhat pedalhat ride bell crash hightom midtom lowtomdrum lines (rim is a cross stick, pedalhat the hi-hat closed by the foot, bell the bell of the ride): X accent, x hit, o soft hit, . silence. A closed or pedal hi-hat stops an open or half open one
                    keyschord stabs: X accent, x stab, - hold the stab, . silence
                    basschord degrees separated by spaces: 1 2 3 5 6 b7 8, - hold, . silence. 3 and 5 follow the chord, so 3 is the minor third of a minor chord and the root of a power chord. b7 is the major seventh of a maj7 chord. Each bar moves by whole octaves to stay between E1 and G3, the range of a 4 string bass
                    >5 >b1approach notes in the bass: > plays a degree of the next chord, in the octave nearest its root, so a walking line leads into the chord change. >b1 is a half step below the next root, or a half step above when that falls below the bass range
                    |starts the next bar of a part; a part shorter than the progression repeats
                    # ...comment, on its own line or after a space (#IV is a numeral)

                    The steps in a bar set its grid. In 4/4 that is 8 for eighths, 12 for triplets or 16 for sixteenths. Swing moves the off beat eighths, or the off beat sixteenths when no part plays eighths. With another progression, each part repeats its bars over the new chords.
